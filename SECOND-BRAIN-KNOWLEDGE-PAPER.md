# SECOND-BRAIN-KNOWLEDGE-PAPER.md — Public Economic Policy Advisor

A curated knowledge base of research papers, books, and authoritative sources underpinning this skill's methodology. These should be consulted (and their principles extracted into skill reference files) when building the actual skill.

> **Note on sourcing:** This list was compiled from general subject-matter knowledge to give the skill a strong research foundation to build from. Before relying on any specific citation (e.g., in an academic, legal, or professional deliverable), verify the exact title, year, and venue independently, as this list has not been individually re-verified against live databases at compilation time.

## Research Papers & Sources (20)

1. R. Musgrave (1959). The Theory of Public Finance. McGraw-Hill — Foundational public-finance framework.
2. A. Atkinson, J. Stiglitz (1980). Lectures on Public Economics. McGraw-Hill — Canonical public-economics theory text.
3. A. Harberger (1962). The Incidence of the Corporation Income Tax. Journal of Political Economy — Foundational tax-incidence model.
4. R. Coase (1960). The Problem of Social Cost. Journal of Law and Economics — Foundational externality/property-rights framework.
5. P. Samuelson (1954). The Pure Theory of Public Expenditure. Review of Economics and Statistics — Public-goods theory foundation.
6. G. Akerlof (1970). The Market for 'Lemons': Quality Uncertainty and the Market Mechanism. Quarterly Journal of Economics — Information asymmetry / market-failure foundation.
7. J. M. Keynes (1936). The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money. Macmillan — Foundational Keynesian macroeconomic framework.
8. M. Friedman (1968). The Role of Monetary Policy. American Economic Review — Foundational monetarist framework.
9. R. Lucas (1976). Econometric Policy Evaluation: A Critique. Carnegie-Rochester Conference Series — New Classical critique relevant to policy modeling.
10. A. Laffer, discussed in J. Wanniski (1978). The Way the World Works. Basic Books — Supply-side tax-policy theory.
11. A. Deaton (2010). Instruments, Randomization, and Learning about Development. Journal of Economic Literature — Critical review of causal policy-evaluation methods.
12. J. Angrist, J.-S. Pischke (2009). Mostly Harmless Econometrics. Princeton University Press — Standard reference on diff-in-diff/RDD/IV methods.
13. E. Duflo, A. Banerjee (2011). Poor Economics: A Radical Rethinking of the Way to Fight Global Poverty. PublicAffairs — RCT-based welfare-policy evaluation approach.
14. A. B. Atkinson (2015). Inequality: What Can Be Done? Harvard University Press — Distributional policy-analysis framework.
15. T. Piketty (2014). Capital in the Twenty-First Century. Harvard University Press — Long-run analysis informing tax/redistribution debates.
16. OECD (2019). Tax Policy Reforms: OECD and Selected Partner Economies. OECD Publishing — Comparative empirical tax-policy evidence base.
17. World Bank (2021). World Development Report: Data for Better Lives. World Bank — Empirical data-driven public-policy evaluation resource.
18. N. G. Mankiw (2020). Principles of Economics (9th ed.). Cengage — Standard framework linking micro/macro theory to policy analysis.
19. J. Stiglitz (2000). Economics of the Public Sector (3rd ed.). Norton — Comprehensive public-sector economics reference.
20. D. Rodrik (2007). One Economics, Many Recipes: Globalization, Institutions, and Economic Growth. Princeton University Press — Comparative institutional-policy analysis framework.
