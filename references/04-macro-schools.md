# 04 — Comparative Macroeconomic Schools (Operational Reference)

> Distilled from Keynes (1936), Friedman (1968), Lucas (1976), Wanniski/Laffer (1978), and Rodrik (2007).

## When to apply
Any macro policy question (stimulus, monetary policy, tax-rate effects, inflation-unemployment trade-offs) where evidence is contested. **Always present multiple viewpoints; never endorse one school as settled.**

## Schools and their positions
- **Keynesian** — active fiscal/monetary stabilisation reduces output gaps; sticky prices/wages justify countercyclical policy. Caveat: debt accumulation, lags, crowding-out debate.
- **Monetarist** — stable money-growth rules beat discretion; inflation is monetary; long-run Phillips curve is vertical. Caveat: velocity instability has weakened simple money targeting.
- **New Classical** — systematic policy has limited real effects under rational expectations; the Lucas critique: structural parameters change under new regimes. Caveat: may understate nominal rigidities.
- **Supply-side** — lower marginal rates and lighter regulation expand the productive base; Laffer-curve effects matter near high rates. Caveat: revenue feedback usually modest at typical OECD rates; can be regressive.
- **Institutional / Post-Keynesian** — institutions, uncertainty, and financial fragility shape outcomes; path-dependence matters. Caveat: harder to formalise.

## Operational steps
1. Identify the policy question.
2. Use the `comparative_schools` tool to retrieve the contrasting positions.
3. Present each school's position, key argument, and caveats.
4. For a pro/con debate, build a balanced matrix from at least two contrasting schools.
5. State that empirical resolution depends on context (elasticities, credibility, the specific economy) and defer to causal-evaluation evidence rather than treating either school as settled.

## Standing cautions
- The Lucas critique: do not assume historical policy-response parameters remain stable under a new regime.
- Always flag time-consistency / credibility of the policy commitment.
