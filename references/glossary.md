# Glossary — Public Economic Policy Advisor

Concise definitions of the terms used across the skill. Keep definitions
operational, not encyclopaedic.

- **Economic incidence** — who actually bears the burden (or captures the benefit) of a tax/subsidy, determined by elasticities, not by statutory assignment.
- **Statutory incidence** — who is legally required to remit a tax/subsidy.
- **Deadweight loss** — efficiency loss created when a wedge drives marginal benefit and marginal cost apart.
- **Externality** — a cost or benefit imposed on a third party not party to the transaction.
- **Public good** — a good that is non-excludable and non-rival; private markets tend to under-supply it.
- **Information asymmetry** — one party has material information the other lacks (adverse selection, moral hazard).
- **Kaldor-Hicks efficiency** — a change is efficient if winners could in principle compensate losers; says nothing about actual distribution.
- **Pigouvian tax** — a tax equal to the marginal external cost, used to internalise a negative externality.
- **Laffer curve** — relationship between tax rates and revenue; revenue may fall at sufficiently high rates, but the effect is modest at typical OECD rates.
- **Lucas critique** — structural econometric parameters are not invariant to changes in policy regime.
- **Parallel trends** — DiD identifying assumption: absent treatment, treated and control outcomes would have followed common trends.
- **Identification strategy** — the logical basis for interpreting a correlation as causal.
- **SUTVA** — Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption: one unit's treatment does not affect another's outcome.
- **LATE** — Local Average Treatment Effect; the effect on compliers identified by an instrument.
- **MCDA** — Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis; structured scoring across weighted criteria.
